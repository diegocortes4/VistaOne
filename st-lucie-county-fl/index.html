About vacant land in St. Lucie County

Port St. Lucie is one of the largest General Development Corporation communities ever platted, covering an enormous area on Florida's Treasure Coast. It has grown rapidly in recent years, which has made its remaining vacant lots some of the most valuable pre-platted parcels in the state.

St. Lucie parcels are valued largely on location within the city, with the northern and southern halves trading differently. Gopher tortoise presence is the most common deduction, and flood zone and wetland status still need checking despite the area's general buildability.

Why St. Lucie County has so many vacant lots

Port St. Lucie is one of the largest General Development Corporation communities ever platted, covering an enormous area of the Treasure Coast. It was sold through the same nationwide campaigns that filled Port Charlotte and North Port, and for decades much of it stayed empty. What makes St. Lucie different from the other GDC counties is what happened next: the area has grown rapidly in recent years, and the remaining vacant lots have become some of the most valuable pre-platted parcels in Florida.

What drives value in St. Lucie County

St. Lucie values are driven primarily by position within Port St. Lucie, with the northern and southern halves trading differently, and by whether the parcel is clean. Gopher tortoise presence is the most common deduction here and a meaningful one. Lakewood Park, north of the city near Fort Pierce, is a separate and considerably more affordable market of quarter-acre and larger lots. Because the area is genuinely growing, buildable parcels here face real builder demand rather than speculative interest.

Who sells us land in St. Lucie County

St. Lucie owners are frequently surprised by what their lot is now worth, because the value moved substantially in a short period and many have not looked at it since they inherited it. Estate sales dominate. We also see owners who held through the 2008 crash, watched the parcel become close to worthless, and stopped paying attention to it entirely, unaware that the market recovered around them.

What we check on a St. Lucie County parcel

  • Wetlands. The most important one. Most builders will not take on wetland permitting, so a wetland parcel is usually not something we can buy.
  • Position within Port St. Lucie, north or south
  • Gopher tortoise activity
  • Flood zone and wetlands
  • Interior neighbourhood lot versus main road frontage
  • Lot size, typically a quarter acre or larger

The St. Lucie County appraiser record and GIS are both good, and flood and wetland layers are reliable. The check that matters most here is gopher tortoise activity, since relocation permitting is a real and recurring cost across Port St. Lucie. We also confirm the parcel sits on an interior residential street rather than fronting one of the main routes, which affects what a builder will pay.

St. Lucie County questions

Is my Port St. Lucie lot worth more than I think?

Quite possibly. Port St. Lucie values moved substantially over the last several years and many owners have not looked at their parcel since inheriting it. Send the parcel number and we will give you a current written figure at no cost, whether or not you sell.

Where is Lakewood Park and do you buy there?

Lakewood Park is north of Port St. Lucie near Fort Pierce. It is a more affordable market of quarter-acre and larger lots, and yes, we buy there.

No. Most owners we work with live out of state. Closing documents are handled remotely with a Florida title company and a mobile or online notary.
