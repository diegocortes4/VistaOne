How to research a Florida vacant lot

Most of what determines whether a vacant parcel is worth anything can be established from public records in about fifteen minutes. The order below is the one we follow, because each step can rule the parcel out before you spend time on the next.

  1. 1
    Pull the appraiser record. Owner of record, mailing address, legal description, acreage, assessed value and sales history. The mailing address alone tells you whether the owner lives near the parcel.
  2. 2
    Check wetlands. This kills more deals than anything else, so do it early. Most builders will not take on wetland permitting at all, which means a wetland parcel usually has no buyer at any sensible price.
  3. 3
    Check the flood zone. Different question entirely, and a much softer one. A flood designation is common across coastal Florida and does not stop a sale. It changes what the parcel is worth and what it costs to insure.
  4. 4
    Establish utilities. County water and sewer, water with septic, or well and septic. In several Florida markets this single distinction accounts for most of the difference in value between two otherwise identical lots. Some counties issue a written availability letter that settles it.
  5. 5
    Check access and road surface. Whether the lot fronts a paved road, a dirt road, or nothing at all. In rural counties this is often the largest single variable in price.
  6. 6
    Check protected species. Gopher tortoise, Florida scrub-jay and burrowing owl all require permitting or relocation before construction, and the cost comes straight off what a builder will pay. County GIS habitat layers give the first indication.
  7. 7
    Check zoning, deed restrictions and balances. Current zoning, any association covering the parcel, and outstanding taxes or dues. Balances are normally settled at closing rather than being a barrier to sale.

Statewide tools

FEMA Flood Map Service Center

The authoritative source for flood zone determination. Search by address or coordinates and pull the FIRM panel for the parcel.

Florida Geospatial Open Data Portal

State GIS layers including parcels, wetlands, land cover and conservation boundaries. Useful when a county's own GIS is thin.

Florida DOR County Property Appraiser List

The Department of Revenue's official directory of every county appraiser and tax collector. The source of record if any link below ever moves.

qPublic (Schneider Geospatial)

The shared platform several smaller Florida counties use instead of running their own site.

Questions about Florida parcel research

What is a Property Appraiser in Florida?

Each of Florida's 67 counties elects a Property Appraiser who maintains the official record for every parcel in that county: ownership, legal description, assessed value, acreage, zoning and sales history. The office does not sell property and does not set tax rates. Its public search is the starting point for researching any Florida parcel.

How do I find the owner of a vacant lot?

Search the county Property Appraiser by parcel ID, address or owner name. The record shows the owner of record and their mailing address, which for vacant land is very often in a different state from the parcel itself.

How do I check if a lot is in a flood zone?

Use the FEMA Flood Map Service Center to pull the FIRM panel covering the parcel. Many county GIS sites also carry a flood layer, and in some counties the local mapping is more current than the federal panel.

How do I find out if a lot has wetlands?

County GIS wetland layers and the state geospatial portal give a first indication, but neither is a formal determination. A wetland delineation by a qualified consultant is the only definitive answer. For most vacant lot transactions the mapped layers are enough to decide whether to proceed.

Is any of this information paid?

No. Florida's public records law makes county Property Appraiser data freely searchable online. Every link on this page is a free, official county or government resource. Paid lookup services repackage the same records.
