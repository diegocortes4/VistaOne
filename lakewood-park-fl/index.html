Why so many Lakewood Park lots sit vacant

Lakewood Park sits north of Port St. Lucie, inland from Fort Pierce, a rural residential subdivision laid out decades ago on the Treasure Coast. It never drew the attention that Port St. Lucie did, which is exactly why a meaningful share of its lots are still vacant and still owned by people who have never walked them.

Lakewood Park is the affordable end of St. Lucie County. Lots run a fifth of an acre and up, and what decides value here is simply whether the parcel is clean: no flood designation, no wet ground, and no gopher tortoises. Because Port St. Lucie values have climbed so sharply, builders have started looking north, and clean Lakewood Park parcels are getting real attention for the first time in years.

What we look at on a Lakewood Park parcel

Not every lot is the same, and the differences matter more on vacant land than most owners expect. When we evaluate a parcel in Lakewood Park, these are the things we check:

  • Wetlands. This one matters most. Most builders will not take on wetland permitting, so a wetland parcel is usually not something we can buy. Worth telling us at the start so nobody wastes time.
  • Flood zone. Parts of Lakewood Park carry a designation. It affects value rather than eligibility and does not stop a sale.
  • Gopher tortoise activity. Common across St. Lucie County. Relocation permitting is a real cost that comes off what a builder will pay, so we check before quoting.
  • Lot size. Parcels here generally start around a fifth of an acre. Larger lots are welcome and easier to place.
  • Interior lot position. Parcels on quiet residential streets are preferred to lots fronting a main road. It is one of the few things you can usually tell us without looking anything up.

You are not expected to know any of this. Send us the parcel ID and we do the research using the St. Lucie County Property Appraiser record, county GIS and flood mapping.

Common reasons Lakewood Park owners sell

Inherited land

A parent or grandparent bought the lot decades ago. Now it sits in an estate, split between siblings, and nobody intends to build.

Out-of-state ownership

You've never seen the parcel, and managing taxes and dues from another state has stopped making sense.

Back taxes or dues

Balances have built up over the years. These are normally cleared at closing from the sale proceeds.

Plans changed

The retirement build never happened, and the lot has become a line item you'd rather close out.

Questions we get most often

How long does it take to close?

Most purchases close in 21 to 30 days through a Florida title company. Estates, trusts and unpaid balances can add time, which is why we ask about them upfront.

Do I have to travel to Florida?

No. Most owners we work with live out of state. Documents are handled remotely with a mobile or online notary.

What if I owe back taxes or association dues?

Very common, and not a barrier. Balances are normally settled at closing out of the proceeds. Tell us the situation so the offer reflects it accurately.

Do I need a survey or appraisal?

No. We do our own research from public records using the St. Lucie County Property Appraiser and county GIS. You are never asked to pay for a survey, appraisal or inspection.

What does it cost me?

No agent commission and no listing fee. Closing costs are handled as set out in the purchase contract.

What if the lot has wetlands or is in a flood zone?

Flood zone and wetlands are two different things. A flood designation occurs in parts of St. Lucie County and does not stop a sale, but it does affect value. Wetlands are much harder, because most builders will not take on the permitting, so a wetland parcel is often not something we can purchase. Tell us upfront either way and we will check the mapping and give you a straight answer quickly rather than leaving you waiting.
