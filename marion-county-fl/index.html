About vacant land in Marion County

Marion County surrounds Ocala in north central Florida and contains several very large pre-platted subdivisions, including Marion Oaks and Silver Springs Shores. Both were sold to out-of-state buyers decades ago and both still hold thousands of undeveloped residential lots.

Marion is the county where utility status matters most. Whether a parcel has a standard water connection available, requires a benefiting-parcel arrangement, or needs a line extension changes what a builder will pay for it. The county issues a utility availability letter that settles the question, and we obtain it as part of our research. Road surface is the other major factor, with paved-road parcels in Rolling Hills and Belleview Heights valued well above dirt-road equivalents.

Why Marion County has so many vacant lots

Marion County's vacant lot stock comes from two very large subdivisions platted in the 1960s and 1970s: Marion Oaks on the southwest side of Ocala and Silver Springs Shores to the southeast. Both were marketed hard to out-of-state buyers at a time when Florida land was being sold as a retirement certainty. The lots sold; the houses largely did not follow. Smaller rural subdivisions such as Rolling Hills, Rainbow Park and Belleview Heights added a further layer of larger, mostly undeveloped parcels across the county.

What drives value in Marion County

Marion is the county where the utility letter decides the price. Whether a parcel has a standard water connection available, is treated as a benefiting parcel, or would require a line extension changes what a builder will pay, sometimes by many thousands of dollars. Road surface is the second lever: across Rolling Hills, Belleview Heights and Rolling Ranch Estates, a paved-frontage parcel is worth appreciably more than the identical lot on a dirt road. Lot size runs from a quarter acre in Rainbow Park up to an acre and beyond in the rural subdivisions.

Who sells us land in Marion County

Marion County sellers skew heavily toward absentee owners who have never seen the parcel. A great many Marion Oaks and Silver Springs Shores lots were bought sight unseen from a brochure and have passed to a second or third generation who inherited a tax bill rather than a plan. We also see owners who bought during the mid-2000s run-up intending to build or flip, and have been paying taxes on a quarter-acre lot ever since without ever breaking ground.

What we check on a Marion County parcel

  • Wetlands. The most important one. Most builders will not take on wetland permitting, so a wetland parcel is usually not something we can buy.
  • Water connection type: standard, benefiting parcel, or extension required
  • Dirt road versus paved road access
  • Lot size, which ranges from a quarter acre up to an acre or more
  • Flood zone and wetlands
  • Power availability at the parcel

Marion County issues a written utility availability letter, which is the only thing that definitively settles the water connection question. Requests go to the county utilities department with the parcel ID, and the letter states whether the connection is standard, benefiting parcel, or extension required, along with any fees attached. We obtain it as part of our research at our cost. The Marion Oaks GIS utility service area layer gives a useful first indication before the letter arrives.

Marion County questions

Why does the utility letter matter so much in Marion County?

Because the fees attached to a non-standard connection come directly off what a builder will pay for the parcel. A lot with a standard connection and the same lot needing a line extension are two different propositions. We request the letter ourselves so the offer reflects the real position rather than an assumption.

Is a dirt road parcel still worth selling?

Yes. Dirt road access lowers the value relative to paved frontage but it does not make a parcel unsellable, particularly in Rolling Hills and Belleview Heights where dirt access is normal for the area.

Do I need to live in Florida to sell?

No. Most owners we work with live out of state. Closing documents are handled remotely with a Florida title company and a mobile or online notary.

What if I owe back taxes or association dues?

Common, and not a barrier. Balances are normally settled at closing out of the sale proceeds. Tell us the situation so the offer reflects it accurately.

Do I need a survey or appraisal?

No. We research the parcel ourselves from public records. You are never asked to pay for a survey, appraisal or inspection.

What does it cost me?

No agent commission and no listing fee. Closing costs are handled as set out in the purchase contract.
