About vacant land in Brevard County

Palm Bay is a large pre-platted community on Florida's Space Coast, developed from the 1960s onward and sold widely to out-of-state buyers. It remains one of the more active vacant lot markets in the state.

Palm Bay is a textbook utility-driven market. A parcel on well and septic, one with city water and septic, and one with full city water and sewer are three quite different propositions to a builder. The county utility map settles which category a lot falls into, and we check it before quoting.

Why Brevard County has so many vacant lots

Palm Bay was platted from the 1960s onward on Florida's Space Coast and sold widely to out-of-state buyers, following the same model as the other large Florida pre-platted communities of that era. It became one of the fastest-growing cities in the state, but the plat was drawn so large that a substantial inventory of vacant residential lots remains, particularly in the southern and western sections furthest from the developed core.

What drives value in Brevard County

Palm Bay is the clearest utility-driven market in our buy box, and the three tiers are unusually distinct. A parcel on full city water and sewer, one with city water and septic, and one on well and septic entirely are three quite different propositions to a builder, with a wide spread between them. The county and city utility maps establish which category a lot falls into. Beyond that, flood zone and wetland status and whether the parcel sits on an interior street do the rest of the work.

Who sells us land in Brevard County

Palm Bay has a high proportion of owners who bought speculatively during the 2000s, when the city was growing fast and lots were traded actively. Many have carried the parcel since without ever visiting Florida. The other common situation is the classic one for the Space Coast: a lot bought decades ago by someone who worked in or near the space industry and planned to retire locally, now sitting in an estate.

What we check on a Brevard County parcel

  • Wetlands. The most important one. Most builders will not take on wetland permitting, so a wetland parcel is usually not something we can buy.
  • Well and septic, city water with septic, or city water and sewer
  • Flood zone and wetlands
  • Interior neighbourhood lot versus main road
  • Lot size, typically around a quarter acre
  • Power availability

The utility map is the first and most important check on any Palm Bay parcel, because it sorts the lot into one of three value tiers before anything else is considered. We pull it before quoting. Flood zone and wetland layers come next, then confirmation that the parcel is an interior lot rather than fronting one of the main roads through the city.

Brevard County questions

How do I find out if my Palm Bay lot has city water?

You do not need to. Send us the parcel number and we pull the utility map ourselves as part of the research. It is the first thing we check on any Palm Bay parcel because it sorts the lot into one of three quite different value tiers.

Are the newer western sections of Palm Bay worth less?

Usually, yes, mainly because they are further from existing utility runs and from the developed core. They are still parcels we actively buy.

Do I need to live in Florida to sell?

No. Most owners we work with live out of state. Closing documents are handled remotely with a Florida title company and a mobile or online notary.

What if I owe back taxes or association dues?

Common, and not a barrier. Balances are normally settled at closing out of the sale proceeds. Tell us the situation so the offer reflects it accurately.

Do I need a survey or appraisal?

No. We research the parcel ourselves from public records. You are never asked to pay for a survey, appraisal or inspection.

What does it cost me?

No agent commission and no listing fee. Closing costs are handled as set out in the purchase contract.
